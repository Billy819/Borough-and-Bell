What this is

This is a timer that helps you work or study in long, calm blocks instead of endless scattered minutes. You work for one hour, you rest for ten minutes, and after four hours you take a proper forty-minute break.

While you work, something else is happening. London is being put back together. Every two hours you finish restores one borough to the map, and lifts the city's health, safety, schools and economy. Sixty-six hours brings all thirty-three boroughs home.

It is a small game, but the work is real. That is the point.

How to begin

  1. Go to the Sitting tab.
  2. Add one task — just one. Write what you will actually do, not what you hope to do.
  3. Press Start and put your phone somewhere you cannot reach it.
  4. When the bell rings, stop. Stand up, look out of a window, drink some water.
  5. Press Start again for the break, then again for the next hour.

That is the whole thing. There is nothing else to learn.

The rhythm: 60 / 10 / 40

60Minutes · SittingOne hour of proper work on one task.
10Minutes · Short breakGet up and move. Away from the screen.
40Minutes · Long breakAfter four sittings. Eat, walk, rest properly.

An hour is longer than the usual twenty-five-minute Pomodoro, and that is deliberate. Deep work — reading, writing, revision, coding — needs a long runway. The first fifteen minutes are usually the hardest. Stay in the chair and it gets easier.

If an hour feels too long today, that is completely fine. Open Settings and change it to whatever you can honestly do. Forty minutes you actually finish beats sixty minutes you abandon.

How the game works

  • Two hours = one borough. Each one arrives as a blue plaque, the same kind you see on London walls.
  • Boroughs come in order, starting with the City of London and Westminster, then working outwards through East, North, South and West London.
  • Five planning regions complete as you go. Central London finishes at 16 hours; the last borough, Hillingdon, arrives at 66.
  • Eight measures rise as you work — human development, quality of life, healthcare, safety, education, tourism, population and GDP. Watch them on The Report.
  • At 66 hours the game ends and London stands as the finest city in the world. You will have done sixty-six hours of real work to get there.

Time counts while the timer is running in a sitting. Breaks do not count towards boroughs, and neither does a paused timer.

Will the timer keep time if I look away?

Yes. The timer does not count seconds one by one — it remembers the exact clock time your session should end and checks the real clock constantly. It also runs on a background worker thread, so it keeps ticking when the tab is hidden.

You can switch tabs, minimise the window, lock your phone, or close the browser entirely and come back later. When you return, the timer will show the true remaining time, and your hours will be counted correctly. Nothing drifts and nothing is lost.

Turn on desktop notifications in Settings and your computer will tell you when the bell rings even if this tab is buried behind others.

If you are struggling today

  • Start absurdly small. Set the timer to ten minutes. Ten real minutes is worth more than a perfect plan you never begin.
  • One task, not five. Pick the single thing that matters most and mark it active. The rest can wait an hour.
  • A broken streak is not a failure. Your hours never reset on their own. The boroughs you have earned stay earned. Come back whenever you are ready and carry on from exactly where you stopped.
  • Take the long break properly. Forty minutes away from the desk is not laziness. It is what makes the next four hours possible.
  • Tired is not the same as lazy. If you have not slept or eaten, fix that first. The work will still be here.

Sixty-six hours sounds enormous. It is two hours a day for a month, or four hours a day for a little over a fortnight. Boroughs arrive steadily the whole way. You are never far from the next one.

Keyboard shortcuts

  • Space — start or pause the timer
  • R — reset the current session
  • S — skip to the next session
  • 1 2 3 — switch to sitting, short break, long break
  • Esc — close any open panel

Shortcuts are off while you are typing in a box.

Where your progress is kept

Everything is saved in your own browser on this device. Nothing is uploaded, there is no account, and no one else can see it. Clearing your browser data — or opening the site in private browsing — will clear your hours too.

You can wipe everything deliberately from Settings → Start again. That cannot be undone, so it asks first.
